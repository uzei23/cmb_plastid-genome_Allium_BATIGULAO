# Lab Answers: Visualize Plastid Genome Structure

## 1. What is the scientific name of your chosen plant and its plastid genome accession number?

The scientific name is *Allium sativum* L., commonly known as garlic. Its complete chloroplast genome has the NCBI RefSeq accession **NC_031829.1**.

## 2. What is the total length of the plastid genome in base pairs?

The total length of the *Allium sativum* chloroplast genome is **153,172 base pairs (bp)**.

## 3. Can you identify the LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions on your map? Briefly describe their positions.

Yes. The circular plastid genome map shows four major regions: the Large Single-Copy (LSC) region, Small Single-Copy (SSC) region, and two Inverted Repeat regions (IRa and IRb). The LSC is the larger single-copy region, while the SSC is shorter. IRa and IRb are duplicated regions that separate the LSC and SSC regions.

The reported region sizes are:

* LSC: 82,035 bp
* SSC: 18,015 bp
* IRa: 26,561 bp
* IRb: 26,561 bp

Together, these regions account for the complete genome length of 153,172 bp.

## 4. Give three examples of genes located in the LSC region.

Three examples of genes located in the LSC region are:

* *psbA*, involved in Photosystem II
* *atpB*, which encodes a subunit of ATP synthase
* *rbcL*, which encodes the large subunit of RuBisCO and participates in carbon fixation

## 5. Give at least one example of a gene located in the SSC region.

An example is *ndhF*, which encodes a subunit of the NADH dehydrogenase-like complex involved in photosynthetic electron transport and related chloroplast functions.

## 6. Give at least one example of a gene that occurs within an inverted repeat region. Is the gene shown more than once because of the duplicated IR regions?

An example is *rrn16*, which encodes 16S ribosomal RNA. It occurs in the inverted repeat regions and is represented by a copy in each IR. Therefore, it is duplicated in the plastid genome.

## 7. Choose one photosynthesis-related gene visible on your map. State its gene name and its general biological function.

The *psbA* gene encodes the D1 reaction-center protein of Photosystem II. This protein participates in the light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis and is important for electron transport.

## 8. What does the direction of the gene arrows or gene orientation tell you about transcription on the plastid genome?

The direction of the arrows indicates the orientation of genes and the direction in which they are transcribed. Genes oriented in opposite directions are transcribed from opposite DNA strands. This shows that transcription in the plastid genome can occur in both directions around the circular genome.

## 9. Look at the GC content graph. Does GC content appear uniform across the entire plastid genome, or does it vary among regions? Describe what you observe without over-interpreting it.

The GC content graph shows fluctuations around the genome rather than a completely uniform pattern. Some regions show higher or lower local GC content than others. The overall GC content measured using Galaxy was 36.68%. These differences indicate variation in nucleotide composition along the genome, but the graph alone does not explain the biological causes of that variation.

## 10. Why is a graphical plastid genome map useful compared with viewing only the raw DNA sequence or a long list of annotations?

A graphical plastid genome map provides a clear overview of the genome's organization. It allows researchers to locate the LSC, SSC, and inverted repeat regions and visualize the positions and orientations of genes. It also displays the distribution of different gene categories and the GC content graph. Compared with a raw DNA sequence or a long annotation list, the map makes structural relationships easier to observe and interpret.
